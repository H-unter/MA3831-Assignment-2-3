\subsection{Data Type Selection}

Several data sources were considered for this investigation. ASX market announcements \cite{asx_market_announcements}, though frequent and direct, are often irrelevant, provided in PDF format unsuited to text mining, and restricted by ASX's terms of use \cite{asx_terms_of_use}. Social media platforms like Twitter and Reddit offer high-volume, unstructured content but suffer from slang, spelling errors \cite{kolasani_twitter_nn}, and questionable reliability \cite{heiden_lstm}.

In contrast, financial news articles are typically factual, well-written, and longer in length. Web-based sources often provide a more structured format and accessible metadata, making them better suited to text mining. It is for these reasons that financial news articles were selected as the primary data source for this investigation. 

\subsection{Website Selection}

\Cref{tab:scraping_websites} shows the comparison of various websites that contain financial and market-related articles, evaluating their suitability for scraping. Copyright is a key legal consideration when using original written works. Under Section 113P of the Copyright Act 1968, Australian copyright law permits educational institutions to copy and communicate works for educational purposes under the 'fair dealing' provisions \cite{copyright_act}.

Websites were assessed based on their suitability for article scraping, with key criteria including explicit permission in terms of use, presence of disallow/allow directives in \code{robots.txt}, use of scraping inhibiting in the form of Cloudflare \cite{does_it_use_cloudflare}, and article structure (e.g. presence of stock codes or site metadata). As shown in \Cref{tab:scraping_websites}, many domains impose legal or technical restrictions on automated data collection, presenting ethical limitations to their use. For example, scraping from websites that explicitly disallow it in their terms of service may breach legal and institutional ethical standards. From a sampling design perspective, sites with structured article metadata and consistent tagging (e.g. Trading Economics, Stock Head) were preferred to ensure replicable and unbiased data collection. Conversely, general news outlets with mixed content and unstructured articles (e.g. ABC News, Yahoo Finance) were excluded due to increased noise and ambiguity in topic association.

\subsection{Web Scraping}

Although \code{robots.txt} did not specify a crawl delay, requests were throttled to one every 3 seconds as an ethical measure to reduce server load. \textit{Small Caps} was selected in part due to its exposed XML sitemap, which simplifies article discovery by listing URLs directly—avoiding the need to emulate navigation behaviour using heavier tools like \textit{Selenium}. Instead, the sitemap is parsed using \code{lxml} with \code{BeautifulSoup}, which is significantly faster and less resource-intensive for static content.

Given the site's predictable structure and absence of JavaScript rendering, \textit{Selenium} was deemed unnecessary. This reduces scraping complexity and improves runtime efficiency. \cref{code:xml_scrape_function} shows the sitemap scraping function, which extracts article metadata including title and last-modified date. As shown in \cref{code:xml_scrape_call}, each paginated sitemap is queried sequentially, filtered for article URLs, and written to a local CSV file. This storage format was chosen for ease of inspection and downstream processing.

\Cref{tab:xml_article_counts} shows the temporal distribution of articles scraped, based on the last modified date in the sitemap.

Once the sitemap URLs were gathered, article content was scraped individually; once again using the \code{requests} library and \code{BeautifulSoup} with the \code{lxml} parser. The article scraping function is shown in \cref{code:article_scraping_function}, where it is seen that the target data with the key metadata resided within HTML containers, enabling reliable extraction of fields such as article title, author, date, sector, body text, and stock codes. 

To allow for incremental scraping and avoid reprocessing, existing scraped articles were tracked via CSV and excluded in subsequent runs. Requests were throttled with a 3-second delay between each fetch to reduce server load. Output was stored locally in a raw CSV format, enabling later augmentation and processing while keeping the collection process transparent and reproducible.
